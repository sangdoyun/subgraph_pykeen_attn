Here is a structured definition of these three levels for the Finance Domain, moving from discrete signals to holistic personas.
The Hierarchy of Financial Pattern Detection
L1: Raw Patterns (The "Atomic" Events)
These are the foundational building blocks. At this level, we move away from continuous, noisy raw data (L0) to discrete, categorized events. We do not look for "meaning" yet; we look for frequency and categorization.
 * Definition: Discrete, repetitive, and categorized data points extracted from raw signals (SMS, App Usage, Location, etc.).
 * Key Transformation: Discretization. Converting exact numbers into bins/ranges to improve generalizability.
 * Why this matters: Exact values (e.g., a salary of $4,321) are sparse and hard to model. Ranges (e.g., Salary $4k–$5k) create repeatable patterns.
Examples:
 * Income: Detecting a Salary Range (e.g., "High Income Bracket") rather than specific credit amounts.
 * Spending: Detecting Merchant Category Frequency (e.g., "3+ Coffee Shop transactions per week").
 * App Usage: Detecting Finance App Installation (e.g., "Has Crypto App" or "Has Loan App").
L2: Composed Patterns (The "Molecular" Habits)
This level aggregates L1 patterns to reveal behaviors and lifestyle habits. Here, we look at the co-occurrence of L1 patterns over time to understand what the user does, not just what data they generate.
 * Definition: A meaningful grouping of L1 patterns that signifies a specific habit, preference, or life stage.
 * Key Transformation: Association & Sequence Mining. Connecting separate L1 events (e.g., Salary Credit + Immediate Transfer to Savings) to find correlations.
 * Why this matters: A single transaction tells you nothing. A repeated sequence of transactions reveals a lifestyle.
Examples:
 * The Commuter: (L1) Daily ride-share deduction + (L1) Metro card top-up \rightarrow Multimodal Commuter Pattern.
 * The Borrower: (L1) Loan App Installed + (L1) Frequent Overdraft SMS \rightarrow Credit-Hungry Behavior.
 * The Socialite: (L1) Weekend Dining range High + (L1) Movie Ticket purchases \rightarrow Weekend Entertainment Habit.
L3: Persona Patterns (The "Organism" Identity)
This is the highest level of abstraction. It combines multiple L2 behaviors to assign a semantic identity to the user. This is the level used for marketing segmentation, risk assessment, or product recommendations.
 * Definition: A holistic label that describes the user's financial psychology and overall lifestyle.
 * Key Transformation: Clustering & Classification. Grouping users based on their collection of L2 habits.
 * Why this matters: This translates data into business language (e.g., "High Net-Worth Individual" or "Gig Economy Worker").
Examples:
 * Investment Focused: Derived from L2 patterns like Regular Saving Habit + Market Watcher.
 * Gig Economy Worker: Derived from L2 patterns like Irregular Income Timing + Multiple Small Credits.
 * Budget Conscious: Derived from L2 patterns like Discount Shopper + Zero-Balance Awareness.

Why "Salary Range" (L1) is better than "Salary Credit"
You mentioned specifically: "We don't say salary credit (raw signal) but salary range."
This is a critical distinction for the algorithm:
 * Noise Reduction: A user earning $5,001 and $5,050 are effectively the same for pattern matching. Grouping them into a "5k–10k Range" makes the pattern detection algorithm (Apriori or FP-Growth) much faster and more accurate.
 * Privacy: It anonymizes the exact financial status while retaining the utility of the data.
 * Stability: L1 patterns become stable features. If a user's salary changes slightly, their L1 pattern remains consistent, preventing model drift.
