1. Why Overlap Happens (And Why Patents Avoid It)

Patterns like:

temporal

sequential

contextual


overlap because they are dimensions, not inventions.

Patents succeed when they define:

> a new unit of behavior representation or system capability,
not just a descriptive category.



So instead of saying:

> “We detect temporal patterns”



you patent:

> “A system that detects triggerable latent routines based on context-conditioned temporal stability.”



That’s the difference.

2. Patent-Worthy Pattern Types (Novel & Useful)

Below are pattern types that are NOT standard, are smartphone-native, and can be framed as inventions.

I’ll name them in patent-friendly language.


---

A. Triggerable Latent Routine Patterns

What it is (novel idea)

Patterns that are:

not always active

not always periodic

but consistently triggered when a latent condition is met


These routines are dormant until the right context appears.

Example

User does not always order food,
but when arriving home after 9 PM, food ordering happens with high probability.

User does not always play music,
but when driving alone, music starts.


Why this is novel

Most systems detect:

frequent actions

or periodic routines


This detects:

> conditionally reliable behavior



Patent framing

> “Detecting latent routines that are activated only under a specific combination of contextual states.”




---

B. Contextual Substitution Patterns ⭐ (Very Strong)

What it is

Patterns where:

different actions substitute for each other

under the same context


Example

At home in the evening:

Sometimes → Netflix

Sometimes → YouTube

Sometimes → Music


The context is stable; the action set rotates.

Why this is novel

Most systems look for:

“action A follows context C”


This captures:

> “Context C produces an interchangeable action set”



Why useful

Better recommendations

Avoids overfitting to one app

Explains variability


Patent framing

> “Identifying interchangeable behavioral actions within a stable contextual trigger.”




---

C. Routine Competition Patterns

What it is

Patterns where:

two or more routines compete for the same time/context

activation of one suppresses the other


Example

Evening at home:

Netflix vs Food ordering


Commute:

Podcast vs Music



Over time, one wins.

Why novel

Most systems track routines independently.
This models competition dynamics.

Patent framing

> “Detecting mutually exclusive behavioral routines competing within overlapping contextual windows.”




---

D. Behavioral Mode-Transition Patterns ⭐

What it is

Patterns that describe how users switch between modes, not what they do inside a mode.

Example

Work → Home → Relaxation

Travel → Arrival → Recovery

Exercise → Cool-down → Media consumption


Why novel

Almost no consumer system models mode transitions explicitly.

Why useful

Perfect for assistants

Enables proactive actions between states


Patent framing

> “Learning and predicting user behavioral mode transitions based on historical state changes.”




---

E. Deviation-Triggered Routines

What it is

Patterns that activate only when behavior deviates from baseline.

Example

User usually cooks at home
→ orders food only when coming late

User usually sleeps early
→ opens social apps only when sleep delayed


Why novel

This is not frequency-based.
It is exception-driven behavior modeling.

Patent framing

> “Detecting routines that are activated by deviations from a learned behavioral baseline.”




---

F. Context-Scoped Habit Strength Patterns

What it is

Measuring habit strength per context, not globally.

Example

Strong habit: Music while driving

Weak habit: Netflix on weekdays

Medium habit: Food ordering on weekends


Why novel

Most systems say “user likes X”.
This says:

> “User habit strength varies by context.”



Patent framing

> “Quantifying habit strength as a function of contextual stability and action reliability.”




---

G. Routine Emergence & Decay Patterns ⭐

What it is

Detecting when a routine:

is forming

stabilizing

fading


Not just detecting its presence.

Example

Gradual increase in gym-related routines

Gradual decline in late-night media


Why novel

Most systems detect what exists, not what is emerging.

Patent framing

> “Modeling lifecycle states of user routines including emergence, stabilization, and decay.”




---

H. Cross-Context Routine Projection

What it is

Detecting when a routine learned in one context projects into a new context.

Example

Music while driving → Music while walking

Food ordering at home → Food ordering while traveling


Why novel

This captures generalization of habits.

Patent framing

> “Projecting learned behavioral routines across distinct contextual domains.”

3. Which of These Are Strong Patent Anchors?

If you want high-confidence patentability, focus on:

1. Triggerable Latent Routines


2. Contextual Substitution Patterns


3. Routine Competition Patterns


4. Deviation-Triggered Routines


5. Routine Emergence & Decay



These are:

Not standard analytics

Not obvious

Very smartphone-native

Highly defensible

Overlap becomes a feature, not a flaw.
