L3: Long-Term Behavioral Insights & Personas

(Built on L2 routines)


---

1. What L3 IS (Technical Definition)

> L3 represents stable, long-term structure over a user’s L2 routines, capturing dominant habits, behavior shifts, and lifestyle tendencies.



L3 answers:

What kind of routines dominate this user’s life?

Are habits changing?

Is the user becoming more / less active, social, frugal, etc.?


Importantly:

L3 does NOT look at raw events

L3 does NOT detect single routines

L3 operates only on L2 pattern statistics over time



---

2. Inputs to L3 (Very Precise)

For each user, we maintain an L2 Pattern Log:

Each L2 pattern has:

Pattern ID
Context signature
Action
Confidence
Timestamp

From this we derive time-aggregated features.


---

3. Core L3 Idea (Key Insight)

> L3 is discovered by modeling the distribution, evolution, and transitions of L2 routines over long time horizons.



So L3 is about:

frequency

dominance

diversity

stability

change



---

4. L3 Feature Construction (Critical Step)

We convert L2 routines into behavioral descriptors.

4.1 Routine Frequency Vector

For a fixed window (e.g., 30 or 90 days):

F = [f₁, f₂, … fₙ]

Where:

fᵢ = normalized frequency of L2 pattern i


Example:

Home→Food: 0.35

Home→Netflix: 0.25

Driving→Music: 0.20

Gym→Music: 0.05

Others: 0.15



---

4.2 Routine Category Aggregation

Group L2 routines into semantic buckets:

Category	Examples

Food	Order food, dine-out
Entertainment	Netflix, YouTube
Mobility	Driving + music
Health	Gym routines
Travel	Airport routines


Compute:

Category_Frequency = sum of routines in category

This creates interpretable lifestyle signals.


---

4.3 Temporal Stability Metrics

Compute:

Entropy of routine distribution
(low = repetitive, high = diverse)

Routine half-life
(how long routines persist)

Dominant routine share


These quantify how structured the user’s life is.


---

4.4 Change & Trend Features (Very Important)

Track deltas across windows:

ΔF = F_current − F_previous

Examples:

Food routines ↑

Travel routines ↓

Entertainment routines ↑ on weekdays


This enables behavior change detection.


---

5. L3 Detection Tasks (What We Actually Compute)

L3 is not a single task — it supports three technical outcomes:


---

5.1 L3-A: Lifestyle Profiling (Persona Detection)

Method

Input: long-term L2 feature vector

Model: interpretable classifier

Decision Tree

RuleFit

Shallow Gradient Boosted Trees



Output

A persona label, e.g.:

Home-centric

Convenience-oriented

Entertainment-driven

Travel-heavy

Highly structured


Why supervised here?

Personas are defined

Business wants control

Interpretability matters



---

5.2 L3-B: Behavior Change Detection (Unsupervised)

Method

Maintain rolling L2 distribution

Use:

KL divergence

Jensen–Shannon distance

Change-point detection



Example

JS(F_last_30_days, F_prev_30_days) > threshold
→ Significant behavior change

Output

“Routine shift detected”

“New habit emerging”

“Old habit fading”


This is very powerful and fully automatic.


---

5.3 L3-C: Habit Lifecycle Modeling

Each L2 routine is assigned a state:

State	Definition

Emerging	New, rising frequency
Stable	High, consistent
Declining	Decreasing
Dormant	Rare / inactive


Method

Time-series smoothing (EMA)

Trend slope estimation


This gives actionable insights without labels.
